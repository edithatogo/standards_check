# CONSORT for Factorial Trials

> Scope: The CONSORT extension for factorial trials provides reporting guidelines for trials that simultaneously evaluate two or more interventions in a factorial design. This extension addresses specific methodological considerations of factorial trials, including the assessment of main effects, interaction effects, and the assumption of no interaction between interventions.
>
> Reference: Kahan BC, Hall SS, Beller EM, Birchenall M, Chan AW, Elbourne D, Little P, Fletcher J, Golub RM, Goulao B, Hopewell S, Islam N, Zwarenstein M, Juszczak E, Montgomery AA. Reporting of Factorial Randomized Trials: Extension of the CONSORT 2010 Statement. JAMA. 2023;330(21):2106-2114.

## Instructions
- Use task list items for checklist boxes; these become interactive checkboxes in PDF.
- Use a span with class `.textfield` for free‑text fields.
- Report all applicable items from the CONSORT factorial extension checklist.

## Title

- [ ] **1a. Identification as a factorial trial:** Indicate in the title that the trial is a factorial trial and specify the number of interventions being evaluated.

- [ ] **1b. Key elements in title:** Include in the title the interventions being evaluated, the comparator(s), the type of trial (factorial), and the population.

## Abstract

- [ ] **2a. Structured summary:** Present a structured summary including, as applicable: background; objectives; methods (including study setting, participants, interventions, outcomes, sample size, randomization, blinding, and statistical methods); results (including participant flow, recruitment, baseline data, numbers analyzed, outcomes, and adverse events); and conclusions.

- [ ] **2b. Trial design in abstract:** Clearly state in the abstract that the trial is a factorial trial and specify the interventions being evaluated.

## Introduction

- [ ] **3a. Scientific background:** Explain the scientific background and rationale for the trial, including justification for using a factorial design to evaluate multiple interventions simultaneously.

- [ ] **3b. Specific objectives:** Clearly state the specific objectives or hypotheses for each intervention and their combinations, including prespecified assessments of main effects and interaction effects.

## Methods

- [ ] **4a. Trial design description:** Describe the factorial trial design and methods used to generate the randomization sequence, including the number of factors and levels.

- [ ] **4b. Important changes:** Describe any important changes to methods after trial commencement (such as eligibility criteria), with reasons.

- [ ] **5a. Eligibility criteria:** Specify eligibility criteria for participants, including any restrictions related to the factorial design.

- [ ] **5b. Settings and locations:** Describe the settings and locations where the data were collected.

- [ ] **6. Interventions:** Clearly describe each intervention and how they were delivered, including details of all intervention combinations; specify which interventions are being evaluated for main effects and interactions.

- [ ] **7a. Outcomes: overview:** Provide an overview of all outcomes measured in the trial, including primary, secondary, and other prespecified outcomes for each intervention and their combinations.

- [ ] **7b. Outcomes: definitions:** For each outcome, provide a precise definition including the measurement variable (what is being measured), method of measurement (how it is measured), metric (how it is summarized), and time point(s) of measurement.

- [ ] **7c. Outcomes: changes:** If any outcomes were modified after the trial commenced, describe the changes and explain why they were made.

- [ ] **8a. Sample size determination:** Explain how the sample size was determined, including assumptions about main effects, interaction effects, and power for each comparison of interest.

- [ ] **8b. Interim analyses:** If applicable, explain any interim analyses and stopping guidelines, including considerations specific to the factorial design.

- [ ] **9a. Randomization sequence:** Describe the mechanism used to generate the randomization sequence, including any stratification or blocking by factors relevant to the factorial design.

- [ ] **9b. Allocation concealment:** Describe the mechanism used to implement the random allocation sequence (such as central telephone; web-based), describing any steps taken to conceal the sequence until interventions were assigned.

- [ ] **10. Blinding:** Describe who was blinded after assignment to interventions (for example, participants, personnel, outcome assessors) and how; explain how blinding was maintained across all intervention combinations.

- [ ] **11a. Statistical methods: overview:** Describe the statistical methods used to compare groups for primary and secondary outcomes, including any methods for adjusting for confounding.

- [ ] **11b. Statistical methods: factorial specific:** Specify methods for assessing main effects and interaction effects; describe approaches for dealing with missing data, multiplicity adjustments, and handling of interaction effects in the analysis.

## Results

- [ ] **12. Participant flow:** Describe the flow of participants, including those who were excluded from the analysis, and reasons for drop-outs and exclusions; provide flow diagrams showing the allocation to each intervention combination.

- [ ] **13a. Recruitment periods:** Report dates defining the periods of recruitment and follow-up.

- [ ] **13b. Reasons for trial end:** Report reasons why the trial ended or was stopped.

- [ ] **14. Baseline data:** Give characteristics of participants at baseline for each intervention combination, including factors that might affect the outcomes.

- [ ] **15. Numbers analyzed:** Report the numbers of participants in each group analyzed for each outcome, indicating whether analysis was by original assigned groups; report numbers for each intervention combination.

- [ ] **16a. Outcomes and estimation:** Report results for each outcome and each group, with confidence intervals and precise p-values; report results for main effects and interaction effects.

- [ ] **16b. Outcomes: factorial specific:** Report assessments of main effects for each intervention and interaction effects between interventions; present results adjusted for these factors as appropriate.

- [ ] **17. Ancillary analyses:** Report results of any other analyses performed, including subgroup analyses and adjusted analyses, especially those related to the factorial design.

- [ ] **18. Harms:** Report all important harms or unintended effects in each group, including any that may be specific to intervention combinations.

## Discussion

- [ ] **19. Limitations:** Discuss limitations of the trial, including potential sources of bias and imprecision, and limitations specific to the factorial design.

- [ ] **20. Generalizability:** Comment on the generalizability (external validity) of the trial results, particularly to other settings where factorial trials might be used.

- [ ] **21. Interpretation:** Discuss the results, taking into account the study's limitations and the results from other relevant studies, particularly those with factorial designs; interpret the clinical significance of main effects and interaction effects.

## Other Information

- [ ] **22. Registration:** Provide the trial registration number and name of the registry; indicate if the factorial design was specified in the registry.

- [ ] **23. Protocol:** Indicate where the full trial protocol can be accessed, including any amendments related to the factorial design.

- [ ] **24. Funding:** Describe sources of funding and other support (such as supply of drugs), and the role of funders.
