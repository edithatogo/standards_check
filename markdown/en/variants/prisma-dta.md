# PRISMA-DTA Checklist

> Scope: The PRISMA-DTA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for Diagnostic Test Accuracy) checklist provides specific guidance for reporting of systematic reviews of diagnostic test accuracy studies. This 27-item checklist addresses title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and other information.
>
> Reference: McInnes MDF, Moher D, Thombs BD, McGrath TA, Bossuyt PM; and the PRISMA-DTA Group. Preferred Reporting Items for a Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Diagnostic Test Accuracy Studies: The PRISMA-DTA Statement. JAMA. 2018;319(4):388-396. doi:10.1001/jama.2017.19163

## Title

- [ ] **1. Identification:** Identify the report as a systematic review (+/- meta-analysis) of diagnostic test accuracy (DTA) studies.

## Abstract

- [ ] **2. Abstract:** See the PRISMA-DTA for Abstracts checklist.

## Introduction

- [ ] **3. Rationale:** Describe the rationale for the review in the context of existing knowledge.
- [ ] **4. Objectives:** Provide an explicit statement of the main objective(s) or question(s) the review addresses.

## Methods

- [ ] **5. Eligibility criteria:** Specify study characteristics (e.g., participants, index test(s), target condition, reference standard) and report characteristics (e.g., years considered, language, publication status) used as criteria for eligibility for the review, giving rationale.
- [ ] **6. Information sources:** Describe all intended information sources (e.g., electronic databases, contact with study authors, trial registers, or other grey literature sources) with planned dates of coverage.
- [ ] **7. Search strategy:** Present the full electronic search strategy for at least one electronic database, including any limits used, such that it could be repeated.
- [ ] **8. Study records: selection process:** State the process that will be used for selecting studies (e.g., two independent reviewers) through each phase of the review (i.e., screening, eligibility, and inclusion in the review).
- [ ] **9. Study records: data collection process:** Describe the mechanism(s) that will be used to manage records and data throughout the review.
- [ ] **10. Data items:** List and define all variables for which data will be sought (e.g., participant characteristics, index test(s), target condition, reference standard, test results, flow and timing, study design characteristics), and any assumptions and simplifications made.
- [ ] **11. Risk of bias in individual studies:** Specify the method(s) used to assess risk of bias in individual studies, including how it will be used in data synthesis.
- [ ] **12. Summary measures:** State the principal summary measures (e.g., sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, likelihood ratios, diagnostic odds ratio, area under the curve).
- [ ] **13. Synthesis methods:** Describe the methods of handling data and combining results of studies, including how the clinical and methodological heterogeneity of studies will be investigated and accounted for.
- [ ] **14. Meta-bias(es):** Specify any assessment of publication bias or selective reporting within studies.
- [ ] **15. Confidence in cumulative evidence:** Describe how the strength of the body of evidence will be assessed.

## Results

- [ ] **16. Study selection:** Give numbers of studies screened, assessed for eligibility, and included in the review, with reasons for exclusions at each stage, ideally with a flow diagram.
- [ ] **17. Study characteristics:** For each study, present characteristics for which data were extracted (e.g., study size, PICOTS, eligibility criteria, participant characteristics, index test(s), target condition, reference standard, time and place of study conduct, test results, flow and timing, study design characteristics).
- [ ] **18. Risk of bias in studies:** Present assessments of risk of bias for each included study.
- [ ] **19. Results of individual studies:** For all outcomes considered (benefits or harms), present, for each study: (a) summary data for each group (where appropriate) and (b) effect estimates and confidence intervals.
- [ ] **20. Synthesis of results:** Present results of each meta-analysis done, including confidence/credible intervals. Present results of any assessment of heterogeneity or inconsistency among the results of included studies.
- [ ] **21. Meta-bias(es):** Present results of any assessment of publication bias or selective reporting within studies.
- [ ] **22. Confidence in cumulative evidence:** Present assessments of confidence in the body of evidence.

## Discussion

- [ ] **23. Limitations:** Discuss limitations at the study and outcome level (e.g., risk of bias), and at the review level (e.g., incomplete retrieval of identified research, reporting bias).
- [ ] **24. Generalizability:** Discuss the generalizability (external validity, applicability) of the review findings.
- [ ] **25. Interpretation:** Provide a general interpretation of the results in the context of other evidence.

## Other Information

- [ ] **26. Registration:** Provide registration information for the review (e.g., PROSPERO registration number).
- [ ] **27. Funding:** Describe sources of funding for the systematic review and other support (e.g., supply of data); role of funders for the systematic review.
