# CONSORT for Harms

> Scope: The CONSORT Harms 2022 extension provides updated guidance for the reporting of harms in randomized trials, ensuring comprehensive and transparent reporting of adverse events and other unintended effects.
>
> Reference: Junqueira DR, Zorzela L, Golder S, Loke Y, Gagnier JJ, Julious SA, Li T, Mayo-Wilson E, Pham B, Phillips R, Santaguida P, Scherer RW, Gøtzsche PC, Moher D, Ioannidis JPA, Vohra S; CONSORT Harms Group. CONSORT Harms 2022 statement, explanation, and elaboration: updated guideline for the reporting of harms in randomized trials. BMJ. 2023;381:e073725.

## Instructions
- Use task list items for checklist boxes; these become interactive checkboxes in PDF.
- Use a span with class `.textfield` for free‑text fields.
- Report all applicable items from the CONSORT-Harms extension checklist.

## Title

- [ ] **1. Identification:** Identify the report as a randomized trial and indicate if harms are a primary, secondary, or exploratory outcome.

## Abstract

- [ ] **2. Structured summary:** Present structured summary including, as applicable: background; objectives; methods (including study setting, participants, interventions, outcomes, sample size, randomization, blinding, and statistical methods); results (including participant flow, recruitment, baseline data, numbers analyzed, outcomes, and adverse events); and conclusions. Include a summary of harms results.

## Introduction

- [ ] **3. Background/rationale:** Explain the scientific background and rationale for the trial, including what is known about the potential benefits and harms of the interventions.

- [ ] **4. Objectives:** Clearly state the specific objectives or hypotheses, including any prespecified hypotheses related to harms.

## Methods

- [ ] **5. Trial design:** Describe the trial design (such as parallel, crossover, or factorial) and methods used to generate the randomization sequence; specify how harms were considered in the design.

- [ ] **6. Participants:** Eligibility criteria for participants and the settings and locations where the data were collected; describe how harms influenced eligibility criteria.

- [ ] **7. Interventions:** Clearly describe the interventions for each group and how they were delivered, including any monitoring for harms.

- [ ] **8. Outcomes:** Clearly define the primary and secondary outcome measures, including how and when they were assessed; provide detailed definitions of harms outcomes, including severity and relationship to intervention.

- [ ] **9. Sample size:** Explain how the sample size was determined and, if applicable, whether it was powered to detect important differences in harms.

- [ ] **10. Randomization:** Describe the mechanism used to generate the randomization sequence, including any stratification or blocking (including by factors that might affect harms).

- [ ] **11. Blinding:** Describe who was blinded after assignment to interventions (for example, participants, personnel, outcome assessors) and how; explain how blinding might affect reporting of harms.

- [ ] **12. Statistical methods:** Describe the statistical methods used to compare groups for primary and secondary outcomes, including any methods for adjusting for confounding or clustering; specify methods for analyzing harms data, including handling of multiple harms and missing data.

- [ ] **13. Harms collection methods:** Describe methods used to collect, monitor, and assess harms data, including standardized tools, frequency of assessment, and whether collection was prospective or retrospective.

- [ ] **14. Harms definitions:** Provide detailed definitions of harms terms used, including severity grading systems, causality assessment methods, and criteria for seriousness.

- [ ] **15. Harms analysis plan:** Describe the planned approach for analyzing harms data, including handling of multiple harms, subgroup analyses, and methods for dealing with rare events.

## Results

- [ ] **16. Participant flow:** Describe the flow of participants, including those who were excluded from the analysis, and reasons for drop-outs and exclusions; include reasons related to harms.

- [ ] **17. Recruitment:** Report dates defining the periods of recruitment and follow-up.

- [ ] **18. Baseline data:** Give characteristics of participants at baseline, including factors that might affect risk of harms.

- [ ] **19. Numbers analyzed:** Report the numbers of participants in each group analyzed for primary and secondary outcomes, indicating whether analysis was by original assigned groups; include numbers analyzed for harms outcomes.

- [ ] **20. Outcomes and estimation:** Report results for each outcome and each group, with confidence intervals and precise p-values; report results for harms outcomes with appropriate detail on frequency, severity, and relationship to intervention.

- [ ] **21. Harms results:** Present results for harms outcomes in detail, including frequency tables, severity distributions, time to onset, duration, and outcomes of harms; include both statistically significant and non-significant findings.

- [ ] **22. Ancillary analyses:** Report results of any other analyses performed, including subgroup analyses and adjusted analyses, especially those related to harms.

## Discussion

- [ ] **23. Limitations:** Discuss limitations of the trial, including potential sources of bias and imprecision in harms reporting, and limitations specific to harms data collection and analysis.

- [ ] **24. Generalizability:** Comment on the generalizability (external validity) of the trial results, particularly regarding harms in different populations or settings.

- [ ] **25. Interpretation:** Discuss the results, taking into account the study's limitations and the results from other relevant studies, particularly regarding harms; interpret the clinical significance of harms findings.

## Other Information

- [ ] **26. Registration:** Provide the trial registration number and name of the registry; indicate if harms outcomes were specified in the registry.

- [ ] **27. Protocol:** Indicate where the full trial protocol can be accessed; include information about harms-related protocol elements.

- [ ] **28. Funding:** Describe sources of funding and other support (such as supply of drugs), and the role of funders; indicate if funders had any role in harms assessment or reporting.
