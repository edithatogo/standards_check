# CONSORT for Non-inferiority Trials

> Scope: The CONSORT extension for non-inferiority and equivalence trials provides reporting guidelines for trials designed to demonstrate that an experimental intervention is not worse than (non-inferiority) or equivalent to (equivalence) an active comparator by more than a pre-specified margin. This extension addresses specific methodological considerations of non-inferiority and equivalence trials.
>
> Reference: Piaggio G, Elbourne DR, Pocock SJ, Evans SJW, Altman DG, for the CONSORT Group. Reporting of noninferiority and equivalence randomized trials: Extension of the CONSORT 2010 statement. JAMA. 2012;308(24):2594-2604.

## Instructions
- Use task list items for checklist boxes; these become interactive checkboxes in PDF.
- Use a span with class `.textfield` for free‑text fields.
- Report all applicable items from the CONSORT non-inferiority extension checklist.

## Title

- [ ] **1a. Identification as a non-inferiority or equivalence trial:** Indicate in the title that the trial is a non-inferiority or equivalence trial and specify the type of comparison being made.

- [ ] **1b. Key elements in title:** Include in the title the interventions being compared, the type of trial (non-inferiority or equivalence), the population, and the primary outcome.

## Abstract

- [ ] **2a. Structured summary:** Present a structured summary including, as applicable: background; objectives; methods (including study setting, participants, interventions, outcomes, sample size, randomization, blinding, and statistical methods); results (including participant flow, recruitment, baseline data, numbers analyzed, outcomes, and adverse events); and conclusions.

- [ ] **2b. Trial design in abstract:** Clearly state in the abstract that the trial is a non-inferiority or equivalence trial and specify the hypothesis being tested.

- [ ] **2c. Non-inferiority or equivalence results:** Report whether non-inferiority or equivalence was demonstrated for the primary outcome, including the confidence interval and the pre-specified margin.

## Introduction

- [ ] **3a. Scientific background:** Explain the scientific background and rationale for the trial, including justification for using a non-inferiority or equivalence design rather than a superiority design.

- [ ] **3b. Specific objectives:** Clearly state the specific non-inferiority or equivalence hypotheses, including the primary and secondary outcomes for which non-inferiority or equivalence will be assessed.

## Methods

- [ ] **4a. Trial design description:** Describe the trial design and methods used to generate the randomization sequence, including details specific to the non-inferiority or equivalence design.

- [ ] **4b. Important changes:** Describe any important changes to methods after trial commencement (such as eligibility criteria), with reasons.

- [ ] **5a. Eligibility criteria:** Specify eligibility criteria for participants, including any restrictions related to the non-inferiority or equivalence design.

- [ ] **5b. Settings and locations:** Describe the settings and locations where the data were collected.

- [ ] **6. Interventions:** Clearly describe the interventions for each group and how they were delivered, including details of the active comparator.

- [ ] **7a. Outcomes: overview:** Provide an overview of all outcomes measured in the trial, including primary, secondary, and other prespecified outcomes for which non-inferiority or equivalence will be assessed.

- [ ] **7b. Outcomes: definitions:** For each outcome, provide a precise definition including the measurement variable (what is being measured), method of measurement (how it is measured), metric (how it is summarized), and time point(s) of measurement.

- [ ] **7c. Outcomes: changes:** If any outcomes were modified after the trial commenced, describe the changes and explain why they were made.

- [ ] **8a. Sample size determination:** Explain how the sample size was determined, including the rationale for the non-inferiority or equivalence margin, assumptions about the effect of the active comparator, and the statistical power for the non-inferiority or equivalence comparison.

- [ ] **8b. Interim analyses:** If applicable, explain any interim analyses and stopping guidelines, including considerations specific to the non-inferiority or equivalence design.

- [ ] **9a. Randomization sequence:** Describe the mechanism used to generate the randomization sequence, including any stratification or blocking.

- [ ] **9b. Allocation concealment:** Describe the mechanism used to implement the random allocation sequence (such as central telephone; web-based), describing any steps taken to conceal the sequence until interventions were assigned.

- [ ] **10. Blinding:** Describe who was blinded after assignment to interventions (for example, participants, personnel, outcome assessors) and how.

- [ ] **11a. Statistical methods: overview:** Describe the statistical methods used to compare groups for primary and secondary outcomes, including any methods for adjusting for confounding.

- [ ] **11b. Statistical methods: non-inferiority or equivalence specific:** Specify the statistical methods for testing non-inferiority or equivalence, including the pre-specified margin, the statistical test used, the confidence level, and the approach to handling missing data and multiplicity adjustments.

## Results

- [ ] **12. Participant flow:** Describe the flow of participants, including those who were excluded from the analysis, and reasons for drop-outs and exclusions.

- [ ] **13a. Recruitment periods:** Report dates defining the periods of recruitment and follow-up.

- [ ] **13b. Reasons for trial end:** Report reasons why the trial ended or was stopped.

- [ ] **14. Baseline data:** Give characteristics of participants at baseline, including factors that might affect the outcomes.

- [ ] **15. Numbers analyzed:** Report the numbers of participants in each group analyzed for each outcome, indicating whether analysis was by original assigned groups.

- [ ] **16a. Outcomes and estimation:** Report results for each outcome and each group, with confidence intervals and precise p-values; report the results of the non-inferiority or equivalence test.

- [ ] **16b. Outcomes: non-inferiority or equivalence specific:** Report the confidence interval for the difference or ratio between groups and indicate whether it falls within the pre-specified non-inferiority or equivalence margin; present results of any sensitivity analyses.

- [ ] **17. Ancillary analyses:** Report results of any other analyses performed, including subgroup analyses and adjusted analyses.

- [ ] **18. Harms:** Report all important harms or unintended effects in each group.

## Discussion

- [ ] **19. Limitations:** Discuss limitations of the trial, including potential sources of bias and imprecision, and limitations specific to the non-inferiority or equivalence design.

- [ ] **20. Generalizability:** Comment on the generalizability (external validity) of the trial results, particularly to other settings where non-inferiority or equivalence trials might be used.

- [ ] **21. Interpretation:** Discuss the results, taking into account the study's limitations and the results from other relevant studies; interpret the clinical significance of demonstrating non-inferiority or equivalence.

## Other Information

- [ ] **22. Registration:** Provide the trial registration number and name of the registry; indicate if the non-inferiority or equivalence design was specified in the registry.

- [ ] **23. Protocol:** Indicate where the full trial protocol can be accessed, including the pre-specified non-inferiority or equivalence margin.

- [ ] **24. Funding:** Describe sources of funding and other support (such as supply of drugs), and the role of funders.
