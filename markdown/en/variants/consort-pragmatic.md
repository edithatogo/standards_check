# CONSORT Extension for Pragmatic Trials

> Scope: The CONSORT Extension for Pragmatic Trials provides additional guidance for reporting pragmatic randomised controlled trials in healthcare. This extension modifies eight items from the standard CONSORT checklist to better capture the unique aspects of pragmatic trial design and implementation.
>
> Reference: https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.a2390

## Instructions
- Use task list items for checklist boxes; these become interactive checkboxes in PDF.
- Use a span with class `.textfield` for free‑text fields.
- This extension modifies 8 specific CONSORT items (2a, 4a, 5, 6a, 7a, 11a, 12a, 20) for pragmatic trials.
- Focus on real-world effectiveness rather than efficacy under ideal conditions.

## Introduction

- [ ] **2a. Background and rationale:** Scientific background and explanation of rationale. **For pragmatic trials, also describe:** the clinical or policy problem being addressed; why the intervention is being evaluated under routine care conditions; how usual care or alternative interventions are currently provided

## Methods

### Participants

- [ ] **4a. Eligibility criteria:** Eligibility criteria for participants and settings where data were collected. **For pragmatic trials, also report:** whether participants are similar to those who would receive this intervention if implemented; whether care providers delivering the intervention are similar to those who would deliver it if implemented; whether the settings are similar to those where the intervention would be implemented

### Interventions

- [ ] **5. Interventions:** The interventions for each group with sufficient details to allow replication, including how and when they were actually administered. **For pragmatic trials, also describe:** who delivered the intervention (and what training or support was provided); how compliance with protocols was monitored and improved; whether there were important differences in protocol adherence between trial and routine practice conditions

### Outcomes

- [ ] **6a. Primary and secondary outcomes:** Completely defined pre-specified primary and secondary outcome measures, including how and when they were assessed. **For pragmatic trials, also report:** whether the outcome is directly relevant to participants, carers, policy makers, and healthcare practitioners; methods of outcome data collection and their differences from routine practice

### Sample size

- [ ] **7a. Sample size:** How sample size was determined. If applicable, description of any interim analyses and stopping guidelines. **For pragmatic trials, also describe:** the smallest worthwhile effect size; if the target difference is smaller than in previous explanatory trials and why

### Blinding

- [ ] **11a. Blinding:** If done, who was blinded after assignment to interventions (for example, participants, care providers, those assessing outcomes) and how. **For pragmatic trials, also describe:** whether participants, carers, intervention providers, and outcome assessors were blinded in ways that differed from routine practice

## Results

### Participant flow

- [ ] **12a. Flow of participants:** For each group, the numbers of participants who were randomly assigned, received intended treatment, and were analysed for the primary outcome. **For pragmatic trials, also report:** the numbers of care providers or centres performing the randomisation; number of patients eligible according to the study protocol

## Discussion

- [ ] **20. Generalisability:** Generalisability (external validity, applicability) of the trial findings. **For pragmatic trials, also discuss:** the extent to which the trial results can be applied to routine care; whether the study setting(s), intervention delivery, and patient population are representative of routine care; what further implementation would require and whether it is likely to be feasible

## Additional CONSORT 2010 Items

*Note: All other CONSORT 2010 checklist items should also be reported as standard. The eight items above are modifications specific to pragmatic trials.*

### Title and Abstract
- [ ] **1a. Identification:** Identification as a randomised trial in the title
- [ ] **1b. Structured summary:** Structured summary of trial design, methods, results, and conclusions

### Introduction
- [ ] **2b. Objectives:** Specific objectives or hypotheses

### Methods
- [ ] **3a. Trial design:** Description of trial design (such as parallel, factorial) including allocation ratio
- [ ] **3b. Changes:** Important changes to methods after trial commencement (such as eligibility criteria), with reasons
- [ ] **4b. Settings:** Settings and locations where the data were collected
- [ ] **6b. Changes:** Any changes to trial outcomes after the trial commenced, with reasons
- [ ] **7b. Interim analyses:** When applicable, explanation of any interim analyses and stopping guidelines
- [ ] **8a. Sequence generation:** Method used to generate the random allocation sequence
- [ ] **8b. Allocation concealment:** Type of randomisation; details of any restriction (such as blocking and block size)
- [ ] **9. Implementation:** Who generated the random allocation sequence, who enrolled participants, and who assigned participants to interventions
- [ ] **10. Statistical methods:** Statistical methods used to compare groups for primary and secondary outcomes

### Results
- [ ] **13a. Recruitment:** Dates defining the periods of recruitment and follow-up
- [ ] **13b. Stopped or ended:** Why the trial ended or was stopped
- [ ] **14a. Baseline data:** A table showing baseline demographic and clinical characteristics for each group
- [ ] **14b. Numbers analysed:** For each group, number of participants (denominator) included in each analysis and whether the analysis was by original assigned groups
- [ ] **15. Outcomes and estimation:** For each primary and secondary outcome, results for each group, and the estimated effect size and its precision
- [ ] **16. Ancillary analyses:** Results of any other analyses performed, including subgroup analyses and adjusted analyses
- [ ] **17a. Harms:** All important harms or unintended effects in each group
- [ ] **17b. Multiplicity:** If relevant, multiplicity and other analyses performed

### Discussion
- [ ] **18. Limitations:** Trial limitations, addressing sources of potential bias, imprecision, and, if relevant, multiplicity of analyses
- [ ] **19. Interpretation:** Interpretation consistent with results, balancing benefits and harms, and considering other relevant evidence

### Other information
- [ ] **21. Registration:** Registration number and name of trial registry
- [ ] **22. Protocol:** Where the full trial protocol can be accessed, if available
- [ ] **23. Funding:** Sources of funding and other support, role of funders
